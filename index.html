Vascular Surgery Field Guide

Compiled by Hashim Salar Medical trainee at the Aga Khan University with an interest in vascular surgery, who put this guide together to make the first weeks on the service easier for whoever comes next.

What you need to be useful on the vascular service: the list and the pager, the operating room and the CTV ICU, the anatomy you will be asked and the notes you will write. Written for residents first, and for every sub-intern and student who joins them.

The vascular mindset

Before the facts, the frame. Vascular is a small, intense service with a logic of its own. Get the logic and the facts hang on it.

Everything reduces to inflow, outflow and conduit. A leg, a graft, a bypass, a fistula, all of it. Is enough blood arriving (inflow), is there a healthy bed for it to drain into (outflow, or runoff), and is the channel between the two intact (conduit)? When a graft clots or a wound will not heal, the question is always which of the three failed.

The vascular patient is a cardiac patient who happens to have a leg or carotid problem. Atherosclerosis is systemic. The person with claudication almost certainly has coronary disease too, which is why the leading cause of death after nearly every vascular operation is myocardial infarction rather than anything in the operated territory. Optimize the heart, and treat the limb in that context.

Time is tissue. Acute limb ischemia, a ruptured aneurysm, acute mesenteric ischemia, a cold mottled foot after a catheterization: these are clocks running against muscle, bowel and brain. The reflex is to anticoagulate early, image fast, and call for help before the window closes.

Pearl

When you present a patient on this service, lead with the vascular bed, the symptom severity, the pulse exam and the hemodynamics (ABI or duplex). An attending wants the limb characterized before the social history. Rounds and presenting turns this into a script.

About this guide

Read me first

This is an educational orientation and study aid for trainees rotating on vascular surgery. It is not a clinical protocol and it is not hospital policy. Thresholds, classifications and trial data drift over time and vary by institution, so check specifics against current society guidelines (SVS, ESVS), your attendings, and local pathways before applying anything to a patient. Logistics such as access and onboarding change often, so confirm day-one details with your clerkship or program coordinator.
