ORIENTATION

The vascular mindset

Before the facts, the frame. Vascular is a small, intense service with a logic of its own. Get the logic and the facts hang on it.

Three habits of thought will make you sound like you belong on the service.

First, everything reduces to inflow, outflow, and conduit. A leg, a graft, a bypass, a fistula, all of it. Is there enough blood arriving (inflow), a healthy bed for it to drain into (outflow, or runoff), and an intact channel between the two (conduit)? When a graft clots or a wound will not heal, the question is always which of those three failed.

Second, the vascular patient is a cardiac patient who happens to have a leg or carotid problem. Atherosclerosis is systemic. The person with claudication almost certainly has coronary disease too, which is why the leading cause of death after nearly every vascular operation is myocardial infarction rather than the operated territory itself. Optimize the heart, and treat the limb in that context.

Third, time is tissue. Acute limb ischemia, ruptured aneurysm, acute mesenteric ischemia, a cold mottled foot after a catheterization, these are clocks running against muscle, bowel, and brain. The reflex is to anticoagulate early, image fast, and call for help before the window closes.

Pearl

When you present a patient on this service, lead with the vascular bed, the symptom severity, the pulse exam, and the hemodynamics (ABI or duplex). An attending wants the limb characterized before the social history. Structure your thinking the way the service thinks and you will rarely be lost. The next chapter turns this into an actual presentation script.

How to use this guide

Read Your first day and Rounds and presenting before you show up, because they are the difference between a smooth first morning and a lost one. Read Foundations and Presentations in the first week, since they cover the language and reasoning everyone will assume you have. Skim the relevant entry in Operations or Endovascular the night before you scrub a given case, then re-read it after while it is fresh. Questions you'll be asked is for the ten minutes before rounds, and Quick reference is what you screenshot and keep on your phone.

How to use this

This guide distills the orientation, anatomy, presentations, operations, and questions that a trainee rotating on vascular surgery is most likely to need. It is deliberately concise and is not a clinical protocol. Read it the night before, skim it on the way in, and let the cases turn the lists into knowledge.
