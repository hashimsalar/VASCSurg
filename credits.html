Field Guide/About

Image credits

Every photograph, scan and illustration in the guide is openly licensed or in the public domain, and every one is listed here with its author, license and source. Where no suitable open image existed, a diagram was drawn for the guide, and its sources are cited instead.

How images were chosen

Images come from Wikimedia Commons, which hosts work that is in the public domain or under a free license, much of it from textbooks such as Gray's Anatomy (1918), open textbooks, US government sources, and clinicians who share their own cases.

  • Licenses. Only public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA and similarly free licenses are used. Each figure's caption names the author and license, with a link to the original file.
  • Changes. Images were resized, converted to WebP, and transparent backgrounds were filled with white so line drawings stay readable in dark mode. Nothing else was changed. Images under CC BY-SA remain under that license.
  • Original diagrams. Topics with no suitable open image, such as aortic landing zones, endoleak types and catheter shapes, have a diagram drawn for this guide. Each one cites the paper or reference it follows. They are schematic and not to scale unless they say so.
  • Patient images. Clinical photographs and scans are ones their authors chose to publish openly, under the licenses shown.

Spot a problem?

If an image is wrongly attributed, or you hold the rights to one and want it changed or removed, tell the guide's author and it will be fixed.

Wires, catheters and devices

  1. French size is circumference-based: divide by 3 for the diameter in millimeters.

    Wires, catheters and devices / Sizes and fit

    Original diagram drawn for this guide. Schematic, not to scale where noted.

    Based on: French (Charriere) scale: outer diameter in mm = French size / 3. Guidewire diameters are stated in inches (0.035 in = 0.89 mm; 0.018 in = 0.46 mm; 0.014 in = 0.36 mm).
