Your first day

The unglamorous logistics that decide whether your first morning goes smoothly. How to get your badge, draw scrubs, log into PowerChart, park, and find the team before rounds start. Sort these out before day one and you can spend your attention on patients instead of paperwork.

Badge Scrubs PowerChart Parking Pager
Confirm the specifics Onboarding steps, office locations, and contact numbers change, and the process differs for medical students, visiting students, and residents. Treat this chapter as a checklist of what to sort out, not as official instructions. Your clerkship coordinator, program coordinator, or the chief resident is the authority on the current process, so reach out to them a week or two ahead.
CHAPTER 01

Before day one

A short email a week or two ahead saves you a frantic first morning. The goal is to arrive already credentialed, already able to log in, and already knowing where to stand.

Email your coordinator or the chief resident before you start and ask the four questions that actually matter: what time and where the team meets in the morning, how you will get a badge and scrubs, whether your PowerChart access is set up, and who your direct contact is for the first day. Everything else can be sorted once you are on the ground.

Pearl

If you are a visiting or away student, the onboarding paperwork (background check, immunization records, HIPAA and EHR training, N95 fit testing) often has to be finished days in advance before your badge and PowerChart login will work. Start it the moment you are accepted, not the week you arrive.

DO THESE FIRST

The day-one checklist

Seven things to have working before rounds on your first morning. Most can be done the day before if you arrive early.

  • Get your photo ID badge Badging / ID office

    Your UNM Hospital badge is also your door-access credential, and at many sites it unlocks the scrub dispenser and parking gates too. Pick it up from the badging office and clip it where it is visible. If a door will not open on day one, your badge access for that unit may not be activated yet, so flag it early.

  • Confirm your PowerChart login UNMH Service Desk · 505-272-3282

    UNM Hospital runs on Cerner PowerChart. Log in once before day one so you are not locked out during rounds. If your username or password does not work, or you need access provisioned, call the UNMH Service Desk; this is the single most common day-one snag.

  • Find out how you draw scrubs OR locker rooms

    Most ORs use a badge-linked automated dispenser (the Scrub-Ex style machines) that issues a set and credits you when you return the used ones. Learn where the men's and women's locker rooms are, how many sets you are allotted, and where to drop dirty scrubs, before you need to change in a hurry.

  • Sort parking or transit UNM Parking & Transportation

    UNM Hospital sits on the university's north campus, and parking is tight. Arrange a permit or a transit plan in advance through UNM Parking and Transportation Services so your first morning is not spent circling a structure. Build in extra time on day one regardless.

  • Set up contact with the team Ask your senior

    Find out how the team communicates, whether that is a pager, secure messaging inside PowerChart, or simply the senior resident's cell number. Give them your number too. Being reachable and responsive is half of being useful as a junior.

  • Clear OR and isolation access Student / employee health

    If you will be scrubbing or seeing patients in airborne isolation, make sure any required N95 fit testing and OR onboarding are done. You cannot mask-fit into an N95 you have never been tested on, and a respirator that does not seal is not protecting anyone.

  • Confirm the schedule Coordinator

    Know your rounding time, where to meet, and whether day one is an OR day, a clinic day, or a consult day. Walking in knowing the plan signals that you take the rotation seriously.

THE EHR

Getting into PowerChart

PowerChart is where you will live: vitals, labs, notes, orders, imaging, and the patient list. The faster you are fluent in it, the more useful you are on rounds.

UNM Hospital's electronic record is Cerner PowerChart. On your first login, do three things before rounds. Find the vascular service patient list (or build a custom list of the patients you are following), open one chart and locate where vitals, labs, ins and outs, medications, and notes live, and practice opening the most recent imaging so you are not fumbling when an attending asks for the CT angiogram.

Patient list

Find or build it

Locate the service list, or make a custom list of your patients so you can pre-round efficiently.

Flowsheets

Vitals & I/O

Know where the vitals flowsheet and the ins-and-outs live; these are the numbers you report first.

Results

Labs & trends

Pull the morning labs and learn to view a trend rather than a single value.

Imaging

Open the study

Practice opening duplex, CTA, and angiography reports and images quickly.

Notes

Read, then write

Read the team's note style, and write yours to match. Do not copy forward stale data.

Orders

Watch first

As a student, understand the orders being placed before you ever pend one. Your level of order access depends on your role.

Red flag · copy-forward

The fastest way to lose trust on rounds is a note that copies yesterday's exam and numbers forward unchanged. Re-examine the patient, pull today's vitals and labs, and write what is true this morning. A wrong number that you reported confidently is worse than no number.

Pearl · locked out

If you are locked out, your password expired, or your access has not been provisioned, the UNMH Service Desk at 505-272-3282 handles login and account problems. Sort it the day before if you can, because the workroom computers fill up at 5 in the morning.

THE OR

Scrubs & the OR

The operating room has its own rules of dress and movement, and getting them right on day one earns you quiet credibility.

  • Drawing scrubs. Use your badge at the dispenser, take only what you need, and return used sets so your account stays in credit. Carry a spare set in your bag for the day a case runs long or messy.
  • Changing and storing. Change in the locker room, not the hallway, and keep your white coat, phone, and badge somewhere you can reach them between cases. Know where to leave valuables.
  • OR attire. Scrub top tucked in, hair fully covered, mask on before you enter a room with an open field, and a fresh mask for each case. Shoe covers and eye protection as your institution requires.
  • Moving in the room. Once you are sterile, keep your hands above your waist and in front of you, do not turn your back to the field, and announce it immediately if you think you have contaminated rather than hoping no one saw.
  • Before you scrub. Introduce yourself to the scrub tech and the circulating nurse, tell them it is your first day, and ask where you should stand. They run the room, and they will help you if you are respectful.
Pearl

The single best way to be welcomed in the OR is to be early, quiet, and clean at the field. The operations chapter walks through how a vascular case actually flows, so read the relevant entry the night before and you will understand why each instrument is being asked for.

THE TEAM

Where to be, who to find

Knowing the shape of the team tells you who to ask for what, and where you fit in the morning.

A vascular service usually runs as a layered team. The attending sets the plan and operates. The chief or senior resident runs the list, the OR, and the consults. Junior residents and interns carry the floor patients and the notes. Students and sub-interns follow a handful of patients closely, pre-round on them, and present. There is often an advanced practice provider who knows the floor and the systems better than anyone, and a coordinator who owns the schedule.

  • In the morning, find the workroom or team room where the list is reviewed, get there before the stated round time, and have your patients pre-rounded and ready to present.
  • For clinical questions, ask the junior resident or APP first; save the chief for decisions and the attending for teaching, unless something is urgent.
  • For logistics, the coordinator and the APP are your people. They have answered every first-day question before.
  • When you do not know, say so plainly and offer to find out. Confident guessing on a vascular service can hurt a patient.
POCKETS

What to carry

A vascular trainee's pockets are slightly different from everyone else's, because the exam runs on one small device.

Essential
Handheld Doppler

The vascular exam lives on the continuous-wave Doppler. Know where the team keeps one, and learn to describe a signal as triphasic, biphasic, or monophasic.

Essential
Marker pen

For marking pulses you found by Doppler so the next person can recheck the same spot. Mark the dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial.

Useful
Trauma shears & tape

For taking down and reapplying dressings on wound and ulcer checks, plus a pen and a small notebook or printed list.
