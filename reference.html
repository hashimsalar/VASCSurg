Red flag

Confirm arterial inflow with an ABI before compressing any leg. Compression of an ischemic limb can cause tissue loss.

Numbers worth memorizing

The figures that come up fast and are expected to be reflexive.

AAA
at or above 3 cm

Infrarenal aorta defined as aneurysmal at 3 cm and above.

AAA repair
5.5 / 5.0 cm

Elective threshold: 5.5 cm in men, 5.0 cm in women, or rapid growth or symptoms.

AAA screening
65 to 75, ever smoked

One-time screening ultrasound for men in this band with a smoking history.

Carotid
at or above 70%

Symptomatic stenosis of 70 to 99 percent has clear benefit from revascularization, ideally within about 2 weeks.

Critical stenosis
50% / 75%

Hemodynamically significant at roughly 50 percent diameter, equal to about 75 percent of cross-sectional area.

Intraop heparin
80 to 100 U/kg

Before arterial clamping, then confirm an activated clotting time above roughly 250 seconds.

Protamine
1 mg / 100 U

Approximate dose to reverse unfractionated heparin; give slowly.

Muscle ischemia
4 to 6 hours

Window before irreversible muscle injury in acute limb ischemia.

Fistula maturity
Rule of 6s

Flow over 600 mL/min, diameter over 6 mm, depth under 6 mm, 6 cm of vein, by 6 weeks.

Aortic branches
T12 to L4

Celiac T12, SMA L1, renals L1 to L2, IMA L3, bifurcation about L4.

Chronic mesenteric
2 of 3 vessels

Symptoms usually need significant disease in at least two of the three mesenteric arteries.

French
1 Fr = 0.33 mm

So 3 French is about 1 mm. Catheter French is outer diameter; sheath French is inner.

Poiseuille
flow ∝ r⁴

Flow varies with the fourth power of the radius, which is why small caliber changes matter so much.
